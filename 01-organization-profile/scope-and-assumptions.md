# Scope and Assumptions

## Purpose

This case study assesses information security risk for a hypothetical security operations environment at Constellis. It is a portfolio exercise built from public information only.

## In scope

- Security operations center (SOC) monitoring and dispatch functions
- Video surveillance, alarm, and access control monitoring systems
- Incident logging, escalation, and handoff processes
- Supporting IT: user accounts and access, endpoints, and network connections used by SOC staff
- Third-party and vendor access to monitoring systems

## Out of scope

- Field operations and armed or protective services
- Government contract details and anything classified
- Any internal or non-public company information
- Systems at individual client sites

## Framework and method

- Control mapping: NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0
- Risk rating: likelihood and impact on a 1 to 5 scale, following the approach in NIST SP 800-30

## Assumptions

- The environment described here is generic to the security operations industry. It does not describe Constellis's actual systems or controls.
- The SOC uses networked cameras, an access control platform, alarm monitoring software, and an incident ticketing tool.
- Staff work in shifts and rely on handoff records between teams.
- Vendors have remote access to maintain monitoring systems.

## Limitations

This is an educational exercise. Findings are illustrative and are not an audit of any real organization.
